Resonant Thrum

Resonant Thrum is a group cardiac-synchrony installation for two to six people. Sitting on a hexagonal grid of haptic tiles and wearing heart-rate monitors, participants share a three-minute ritual in which their heartbeats become the room's sound and light. As the group's hearts converge, the space grows resonant and warm.

Proposed for the Ars Biologica ArtXScience Residency — Ars Electronica × Če­ské Budějovice, European Capital of Culture 2028.

Sit together for three minutes and watch living water light up to your shared heartbeat.

What I am proposing

For the residency I am extending the work beneath the human. A living floor of bioluminescent marine dinoflagellates sits at the centre of the piece. The haptic pulse of the group's collective heartbeat reaches the water as vibration, and the cells answer with light through mechanotransduction.

The exchange runs both ways. A pulsing grow light and the group's exhaled breath feed the culture's photosynthesis, and the energy it stores is what powers the light it gives back. These cells build their glow from the chlorophyll they grow across the run of the show, so the work is kept alive by the light and breath of everyone who visits.

The science

Working with the Institute of Hydrobiology at the Biology Centre of the Czech Academy of Sciences, the piece takes quorum sensing — the way microbes act as one once they cross a threshold — as a mirror of human cardiac synchrony, which itself predicts group cohesion and collective decision-making. It stages the room as a single more-than-human body.

The work it grows from

Resonant Thrum evolves from Resident Thrum, which premiered at ACM Creativity & Cognition in 2026. See the Resident Thrum project page for the installation and the research behind it.
