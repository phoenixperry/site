Humanising Technology

Phoenix Perry is an interdisciplinary researcher, educator, and practitioner specializing in the intersections of human-computer interaction (HCI), creative computing, and machine learning (ML) for embodied and artistic interaction design. Their work spans creative technology, games, immersive media, interactive machine learning, and generative AI, with a strong focus on empowering creative practitioners through accessible tools and methodologies.

Current Grants

The Antigravity Machine

Project Lead · UKRI AHRC with SSHRC, AI Humanities Sandpit · 2026–27

Large AI models pull every artist towards the same average answer. This UK and Canadian project builds open-source tools that let artists steer models towards their own work and culture, using gesture and movement, designed with disabled artists through crip design. It ends with an installation at Factory International, Manchester, and a performance at the Edinburgh Fringe in 2027.

Federated Data Commons for Creative Communities

Project Lead · UKRI AHRC, Bridging Responsible AI Divides (BRAID) · 2026–27

Can artists share their work without giving up control of it? This UK and US project, with NYU, is building Held in Common: a commons where creative data lives on nodes its makers hold, and the communities who make it decide how it is governed. Its first public output is a model landscape showing how open “open” AI models really are.

Recent Papers

Cripping the Controller

Best Paper Award, CHI PLAY 2026

Perry, P., Fiebrink, R. & Grierson, M. (2026). Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 10(7), CHI PLAY.

Three disabled designers led the making of new game controllers alongside three Alt Ctrl developers. Over five months each pair built a new controller, from a modular tube controller to a plush sound toy and a set of networked kettles. The paper sets out guidelines for crip game design, which treats disability as a source of method rather than a problem to fix.

Crip Design for Collaborative Musical Interfaces

Perry, P. (2026). Iterative Development of Bot Party, a Touch-Based Sonic Game. NIME 2026, London.

A look back at seven years of design changes and fifteen exhibitions of Bot Party, a musical game you play by touching other people. The paper argues that design led by disabled people's knowledge creates new kinds of musical interaction, rather than adapting old ones.

Read the paper

Resident Thrum

Perry, P. (2026). Designing for Group Cardiac Synchronization through Multimodal Biofeedback. ACM Creativity & Cognition 2026.

An installation for up to six people. Sensors read each player's heart rate, and the hexagonal tiles vibrate, sound and light up as the group's heartbeats move closer together. About 100 people took part over three days at the conference art exhibition.

Other Funded Projects

Music RAI

This 12-month project funded by UKRI EPSRC addresses the bias in AI music generation that results from an over-reliance on datasets of Western classical and pop music. By creating AI tools and datasets that enhance access to underrepresented genres, it aims to promote inclusivity in music consumption and empower diverse musical expression.
