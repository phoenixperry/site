Organisations

For over 30 years, I have fostered strategic communities that unite creators to share ideas, collaborate, and inspire more humane futures through technology.

Phoenix Perry builds places where new people get to make technology: a gallery in Brooklyn, a free coding school for women and non-binary people, and the degrees at a creative computing institute in London. Together, Code Liberation and Devotion Gallery reached more than 20,000 people.

Code Liberation Foundation

Founder, 2013 to 2021

Free game-making classes for women and non-binary people, with no experience needed. Phoenix launched Code Liberation on International Women’s Day 2013 and ran it for eight years, from New York to London, with workshops at the V&A and a FutureLearn course. More than 40 free classes are published online.

Devotion Gallery, Brooklyn

Owner and artistic director, 2009 to 2014

Art, science and new media in a Williamsburg storefront. Over five years Devotion held more than 200 events and 35 exhibitions, showing more than 80 artists, and became Brooklyn’s first Ableton Certified Training Facility. Wired, Boing Boing and NPR covered the work, and a case study of the gallery was presented at ACM CHI 2013.

The Devotion story

UAL Creative Computing Institute

Course designer and leader, 2018 to now

Phoenix helped establish the Creative Computing Institute at University of the Arts London and designed its two degrees, the MSc and the BSc in Creative Computing. That meant writing about 70% of the module specifications and taking both degrees through validation. Phoenix then led them: the MSc as course leader, and the BSc through its first year. Under Phoenix, the MSc grew from 17 students to 98. Phoenix also founded Machines We Imagine, a UAL research group for artists who build their own tools.
