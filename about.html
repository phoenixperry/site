Cult Hero

Dr Phoenix Perry is an artist, researcher and activist who has spent more than 25 years making technology that brings people together. They are Reader in Games and Creative Technologies at the UAL Creative Computing Institute in London, where they designed the MSc and BSc in Creative Computing, and Project Lead on Federated Data Commons for Creative Communities, an AHRC-funded project with New York University.

Their work spans games, embodied interaction, accessible design and creative AI, and keeps coming back to who gets to shape technology and how play can make it fairer. Phoenix sees building schools, studios and communities as a kind of social fiction. What world do we want? How do we get there from here?

The story so far

San Francisco, 1997 to 2002

It started with video. In 1997 Phoenix was artistic director of Artists’ Television Access, and their single-channel work went on to screen at the Oberhausen Short Film Festival, LACMA, the Yerba Buena Center for the Arts and the New York Video Festival. In 2000, FLYER and XLR8R named Phoenix the best video artist in San Francisco.

In 2001 Phoenix and Brian Jackson started Synth, a monthly club night where live electronic music and video art shared the room. Phoenix and Scott Pagano were its resident video artists, and the bookings read like a history of electronic music: Kid606, Scanner, Lusine, Jonas Bering, Gold Chains, Kit Clayton and Mr. Velcro Fastener, with Miss Kittin and The Hacker on the bill for the Great American Music Hall. Synth won Best Club to Hear the Future from the SF Bay Guardian and Best Pickup Spot for HAL 9000 from SF Weekly. Phoenix, Brian and Jenny Young also played as Memory Systems on Form Records. With Scott, Phoenix made Reline, a DVD series of artists bending software and hardware, which screened at the Guggenheim Bilbao, Lincoln Center and Yerba Buena.

New York, 2002 to 2015

In Brooklyn, Phoenix opened Devotion Gallery (2009 to 2014) with a board that included Brian Jackson, Margaret Schedel, David Last and Phillip Stearns. Devotion showed Sougwen Chung, whose work is now in the Whitney’s collection, Scott Draves of Electric Sheep, Jeremy Bailey, Bethany Shorb and Nicole Aptekar; Babycastles brought JODI; Robert Henke played as Monolake; Bruce Sterling wrote about it in Wired. It became Brooklyn’s first Ableton Certified Training Facility.

Phoenix was teaching at NYU by then, co-wrote Meet the Kinect (Apress, 2011), and used machine learning in art with Margaret Schedel and Rebecca Fiebrink on #000000Swan (NIME 2011). In 2013 Phoenix founded the Code Liberation Foundation with Catt Small, Jane Friedhoff, Nina Freeman, Kay and Kaira Villanueva, ran workshops with Black Girls Code, and took Crystallon to the PlayStation booth at GDC and E3. Phoenix also co-founded the game studio Dozen Eyes, making work for the US State Department and the New York Historical Society.

London, 2015 to now

Phoenix moved to London and created the MA in Independent Games and Playable Experience Design at Goldsmiths, from the business case and validation to leading the course. Alongside it, Phoenix brought Code Liberation to the V&A with the Processing Foundation and built Bot Party, which played GDC Alt.Ctrl, Somerset House and IndieCade and earned an IGF nomination. Then came TEDxLiège, a panel at Resonate with Memo Akten and Rebecca Fiebrink, and the keynote that opened Zach Lieberman’s Future Sketches talks at the MIT Media Lab.

In 2018 Phoenix helped establish the UAL Creative Computing Institute and designed its MSc and BSc. With Rebecca Fiebrink, Marco Gillies, Nicola Plant, Clarice Hilton, Carlos González Díaz, Ruth Gibson and Bruno Martelli, Phoenix built InteractML, now used by more than 60,000 developers. A PhD at Goldsmiths followed in 2024, then the Future Days keynote in Lisbon, and in 2026 two AHRC-funded projects and the Best Paper Award at CHI PLAY for Cripping the Controller. The future, it turns out, has usually been somewhere nearby.

Highlights

  • Has secured about £1.23 million in research funding from the AHRC, SSHRC, EPSRC, UKRI, Google and Epic Games. In 2026 Phoenix became Project Lead on two AHRC-funded projects, with UAL as lead organisation: The Antigravity Machine, a UK and Canada collaboration co-funded by SSHRC, and Federated Data Commons for Creative Communities (AHRC BRAID).
  • Best Paper Award at ACM CHI PLAY 2026 for Cripping the Controller, with new 2026 papers at NIME and ACM Creativity & Cognition. More than 15 peer-reviewed papers and contributions to four books, including Meet the Kinect (Apress) and Code as Creative Medium (MIT Press).
  • Creator of InteractML, open-source interactive machine learning for Unity and Unreal, used by more than 60,000 developers.
  • Founder of the Code Liberation Foundation, which taught creative computing to 6,000 women (2013 to 2021), and owner and artistic director of Devotion Gallery in Brooklyn (2009 to 2014).
  • More than 70 talks and keynotes in Europe, the UK, the US, Canada and South Africa, including Future Days, KIKK, the Gotland Game Conference, IndieCade, TEDxLiège and the Sir Run Run Shaw Lecture at Stony Brook University.
  • Exhibiting since 1998: Bot Party at GDC Alt.Ctrl, Somerset House and IndieCade; Forest Daydream at the Wellcome Collection; Resident Thrum at ACM Creativity & Cognition; and early video work at Oberhausen, LACMA and Yerba Buena Center for the Arts.
  • Helped establish the UAL Creative Computing Institute and has designed and led degrees at UAL and Goldsmiths, after teaching at NYU, UCL and the Utrecht School of the Arts. Senior Fellow of the Higher Education Academy.
  • Serves on the ACM Games editorial board and the Processing Foundation advisory board, and is Artworks Co-Chair for ACM Creativity & Cognition 2026.
  • Before academia, 14 years as an art director and creative director in interactive design (1999 to 2013), and co-founder of the game studio Dozen Eyes in New York.

A short bio you are welcome to use:

Dr Phoenix Perry is an artist, researcher and Reader in Games and Creative Technologies at the UAL Creative Computing Institute in London. Their work explores play, embodied interaction and fairer AI. They lead two AHRC-funded projects, The Antigravity Machine and Federated Data Commons for Creative Communities, founded the Code Liberation Foundation, created the open-source toolkit InteractML, and have given more than 70 talks worldwide. Their 2026 paper Cripping the Controller won Best Paper at ACM CHI PLAY.

Pronouns: they/them. For interviews, talks, commissions and research partnerships, email phoenix@phoenixperry.com. Photo by Julian Dasgupta.
