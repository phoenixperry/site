A Future Worth Playing For

Phoenix Perry makes games you play by touching strangers, floors that sync a room's heartbeats, and AI tools that answer to artists instead of platforms. For more than a decade Phoenix has taken that work on stage to argue one thing: technology gets better when it is designed for bodies, for communities and for joy.

Keynotes at the Audio Developer Conference, Future Days Lisbon, the Gotland Game Conference, MIT Future Sketches, IndieCade and the Open Hardware Summit. A TEDx talk in Liège. More than 70 talks in Europe, the UK, the US, Canada and South Africa.

Talks Phoenix gives

Who gets to shape AI?

Generative AI pulls every artist towards the same average answer. Phoenix leads two funded projects pushing back: The Antigravity Machine, which lets artists steer models with movement, and Held in Common, a commons where creative data stays with the people who made it.

Cripping the controller

What happens when disabled designers lead? Drawing on 20 years of building custom controllers and a Best Paper Award at CHI PLAY 2026, Phoenix shows how disability-led design produces better, stranger and more joyful technology for everyone.

Play as human connection

In Bot Party, strangers make music by holding hands. In Resident Thrum, a group learns to share a heartbeat. Phoenix explains how physical play can rebuild the connection screens wear away.
